ILIA Email Management System

One place for NIW admins to see, understand, and update every email ILIA sends: what it says, who receives it, what triggers it, which address it comes from, and whether it's still in use. Copy changes would no longer need a developer. This page is the living project overview, built one tab at a time.

→ Why this project exists, in plain language. Based on a first-pass review of the ILIA code and the current email spreadsheet (Oct 2026).

The problem in one sentence

ILIA sends dozens of different emails to clients, agents, giftors, trustees, IMOs and carriers, but nobody can see them all in one place. The wording is buried in code, scattered across three systems, styled inconsistently, and tracked by hand in a spreadsheet that drifts from reality. Changing a single sentence requires a developer.

Spreadsheets & pages

A many-tab spreadsheet plus one-off inventory pages, all kept up by hand. They're the only "maps" we have, and they don't match the code.

✕ None of these are connected, and none of them can be viewed or edited by an admin. Email copy, action items and in-app notifications for the same event also live in separate places.

Zoom in: payments alone

A recent inventory of just the Payments Center found about 28 payment-related emails sent by 3 different systems. They look different, some events send two overlapping emails, and the communications log can only say "Queued" or "Failed", never whether an email was actually delivered. That's one workflow; ILIA has many.

What that causes

1

No single list of our emails

To answer "what emails do we send a giftor?" someone has to dig through code or a multi-tab spreadsheet, and neither is complete.

ExampleSome emails in the spreadsheet don't exist in code. Some emails in code aren't in the spreadsheet.
2

Every wording change needs a developer

Subject lines and body copy are written directly into code. Fixing a typo or updating a date means a ticket, a sprint slot, and a release.

ExampleAn office-move notice and "pilot program" language are still hard-coded in live templates.
3

Emails don't look like one brand

There's no shared header, footer, or logo. Fonts, colours, and sign-offs differ from email to email.

ExampleEmails sign off as "MyIlia", "Simplicity-NIW", "Ilia Team", or nothing at all.
4

Too many "from" addresses

Different emails come from different no-reply senders, and the two systems disagree on which address to use, which confuses recipients and hurts deliverability.

ExampleThe old and new systems are set up with different default sender addresses.
5

We can't tell what's alive and what's dead

Many templates have no active trigger, and others are only half-wired. Nobody knows which are safe to retire and which clients still receive.

ExampleSeveral templates in the newer system are never called by anything.
6

No record of what was sent

ILIA doesn't keep a history of the emails it sends, or whether they were delivered or bounced. When a client says "I never got it," support has nothing to check.

ExampleGaps were found in the payment reminder logs for the 90, 60 and 30-day notices. Bounce notices from the email provider are received but not saved.
7

Copy quality slips through

With no preview or review step, typos, unfilled placeholders, and "please reply" lines on no-reply addresses reach real clients. The preview an admin sees can differ from what's actually sent.

ExampleSome payment emails go out with bracketed placeholder text instead of the real word.
8

One event, many disconnected messages

A single event can create an email, an action item and an in-app notification, and each is built and stored separately. No one can see everything a client receives for that event in one view.

ExampleA GWT giftor can receive two different "payment" emails for the same payment.

Today vs. where we want to be

Today

  • Emails spread across three systems, the code, and hand-kept spreadsheets
  • Copy changes need a developer and a release
  • Inconsistent design and sign-offs
  • Multiple, conflicting sender addresses
  • Unknown which emails are active or dead
  • No send / delivery history

The goal

  • One admin screen listing every email, grouped by workflow, with the action items and notifications each event creates
  • See the real copy, recipient type, trigger and sender for each
  • Admins edit copy themselves, with preview and approval
  • One shared, on-brand email design
  • Each email clearly marked Active or Retired
  • A history of what was sent, to whom, and whether it arrived
